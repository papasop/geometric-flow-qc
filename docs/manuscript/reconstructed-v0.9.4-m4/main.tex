\begin{abstract}
We study a synthetic, exact state-vector model of Shor order finding in which
the ideal phase-estimation response is held fixed while implementation-noise
coordinates are varied along a four-dimensional exact-response fibre.  On a
frozen six-instance benchmark with 72 meta-records, intrinsic Euclidean ascent
on this fibre improves held-out exact-order recovery relative to the unchanged
reference, a basis-invariant random tangent comparator, and an SPSA comparator
run under the same composite response-call ledger.  In the v0.9.4-r1
corrective archive, the equal-instance mean gains are
$4.937962211018602\times 10^{-4}$ over reference,
$3.411545293638851\times 10^{-4}$ over random search, and
$1.2279600467454457\times 10^{-4}$ over SPSA, with paired hierarchical
bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals. In comparator order (reference, random,
SPSA), these are
\begin{gather*}
[2.902978897001541\times 10^{-4},\,6.822119290361299\times 10^{-4}],\\
[2.3521176375243113\times 10^{-4},\,4.5502493973362636\times 10^{-4}],\\
[5.996365059637254\times 10^{-5},\,2.0092595128065157\times 10^{-4}].
\end{gather*}
The intrinsic method beats SPSA on 72/72 records and has positive
instance-level means on 6/6 instances; it beats the random comparator on 68/72
records and has positive instance-level means on 6/6 instances.  These results
support navigability of the stipulated fibre under the declared synthetic
noise family only.  They are not evidence of a native hardware advantage,
fault-tolerant resource reduction, cryptographic-scale factoring, practical
computational advantage, or an asymptotic improvement to Shor's algorithm.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Shor order finding reduces factoring to estimating the period of modular
exponentiation.  In the ideal algorithm, the relevant response is the
phase-estimation distribution induced by an order $r$ and a control register
of size $M=2^m$.  Practical implementations, however, introduce coherent and
stochastic distortions before the exact-order decision rule is applied.  The
question addressed here is narrow: within a declared non-native synthetic
noise ansatz, can one move in directions that preserve the ideal response while
improving the held-out exact-order recovery probability?

The experiment studies exact-response fibres rather than new asymptotic
algorithms.  A fibre is the set of parameter perturbations that leave the
declared ideal response invariant to first order.  The intrinsic method moves
inside a four-dimensional tangent fibre and optimizes the held-out task
objective.  The frozen benchmark compares it with an unchanged reference,
basis-invariant random tangent search, and SPSA.  Revision v0.9.4-r1 is a
reproducibility correction: it pins the analytic tangent-coordinate basis used
by SPSA.  The original v0.9.4 tangent subspace was correct, but the concrete
coordinate basis used inside the degenerate four-dimensional subspace was not
archived; this matters for coordinate Rademacher perturbations.

\section{Model And Exact-Response Fibre}

\subsection{Phase-Estimation Response}

For an instance $(N,a)$ with multiplicative order $r$, the ideal
phase-estimation amplitudes are indexed by a control-register outcome
$y\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$ and by an eigensector $s$.  The reconstructed formula
uses the normalization implemented by the archive:
\begin{equation}
A_s(y;\theta,\xi)
= \frac{1}{M}\sum_{x=0}^{M-1}
\exp\left[
2\pi i x\left(\frac{s}{r}-\frac{y}{M}\right)
+ i\phi_x(\theta,\xi)
\right].
\end{equation}
The exact-order success probability is obtained by summing the probabilities
of outcomes whose continued-fraction post-processing recovers the exact
order.  The code retains final normalization as numerical insurance, not as a
mathematical correction to the amplitude definition.

\subsection{Synthetic Implementation Ansatz}

The archive uses a shared nine-parameter synthetic implementation/noise
ansatz.  Its declared response matrix has rank five and a four-dimensional
kernel.  The sixth response coordinate is an explicit consistency coordinate,
\begin{equation}
r_6 = r_1 + r_2 + r_3 + r_5 .
\end{equation}
Consequently, the tangent subspace has dimension four.  This subspace is the
object preserved by the original v0.9.4 archive.

\subsection{Pinned Analytic Tangent Basis}

The corrective r1 archive makes the SPSA coordinate convention explicit.  The
tangent basis is fixed to the following orthonormal columns in
$\mathbb{R}^9$:
\begin{align}
t_1 &= (e_0+e_3)/\sqrt{2},\\
t_2 &= (e_1-e_5)/\sqrt{2},\\
t_3 &= (e_1+e_5-2e_6)/\sqrt{6},\\
t_4 &= (e_2-e_8)/\sqrt{2}.
\end{align}
The deposited basis hash is

The archived residuals are $2.4655887277772096\times10^{-16}$ for the Gram
check, $0$ for the response check, and
$3.895959316580178\times10^{-16}$ for the projector check.

\section{Protocol}

The frozen instance family is
$(15,2)$, $(15,7)$, $(21,2)$, $(21,5)$, $(33,2)$, and $(35,2)$, with exact
orders $4$, $4$, $6$, $6$, $10$, and $12$.  Each instance has 12 meta-seeds.
Each record uses five training seeds and eight shifted held-out seeds.  The
primary statistic is the equal-instance-weight mean held-out gain, with paired
hierarchical bootstrap intervals obtained by resampling instances and then
meta-records.  All methods are compared under an equal composite response-call
ledger of 5520 calls per record.  The intrinsic method uses 5500 task calls
plus 20 padding calls, whereas the random and SPSA baselines use 5520 task
calls.

\section{Interpretation And Boundaries}

The result is a reproducible empirical separation inside a declared synthetic
model.  It shows that exact-response fibres can contain task-relevant
directions even when the ideal response is held fixed.  It does not show that
the same directions exist in a native compiled circuit, that a hardware
implementation would benefit, that fault-tolerant resources are reduced, that
cryptographic-scale factoring becomes easier, or that Shor order finding has
an improved asymptotic complexity.  The appropriate interpretation is
navigability of a non-native exact-response fibre, not practical computational
advantage.

\section{Reproducibility Correction}

The v0.9.4-r1 archive retains the original v0.9.4 driver, protocol, result,
stdout, tag, and release unchanged, and adds a corrective driver, explicit
tangent basis, environment/protocol files, and a full 72-record result JSON.
The correction does not attempt to recreate the undeclared v0.9.4 SPSA
coordinate basis.  It instead defines the SPSA coordinates explicitly and
reruns the full 72-record benchmark.  The r1 SPSA values are therefore not a
bitwise reproduction of the old SPSA archive values.
